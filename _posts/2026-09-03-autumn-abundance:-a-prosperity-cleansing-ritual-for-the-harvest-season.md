There's a reason the air feels different once September settles in. The light shifts, the mornings turn crisp, and something in us starts craving order — a clean slate before the world outside begins to gather itself in for winter. This restlessness isn't just seasonal affective whimsy. It's ancient. Harvest season has always been tied to abundance: reaping what you've sown, taking stock of what you have, and making sure your stores are full before the dark months arrive.

This is the heart of an autumn prosperity ritual. Before you reach for the pumpkins and string lights, there's earlier work to do — work that clears the way for abundance to actually take root. Think of it less like seasonal chores and more like tending the soil before you plant. You wouldn't scatter seeds on hard, cluttered ground and expect a harvest. Your home works the same way.

Below is a three-part ritual you can adapt to your own space, practice, and pace: deep cleaning as energetic reset, botanicals as prosperity magic, and decor as the visible bridge from cozy autumn into full Halloween season.

## Part One: The Deep Clean & Declutter

In folk magic traditions across many cultures, physical clutter is treated as a mirror of energetic and financial stagnation. A junk drawer that hasn't been touched in a year, closets stuffed with things you don't use, dust gathering in corners — these aren't just untidy, they're symbols of stuck energy. Money and opportunity are said to move the way air and light move: freely, or not at all.

Start with your entryway. In many traditions, the entry is treated as the threshold through which fortune enters a home — so if it's cluttered with shoes, mail, and forgotten packages, that's the first place to clear. From there, work room by room, but don't feel pressure to deep-clean every inch of your home in one weekend. Pick the spaces you actually spend time in and pass through daily.

Many practitioners also observe a directional separation between banishing and drawing: sweeping or wiping *outward* (widdershins, counterclockwise) to expel old energy, and — once that pass is fully dry — washing or moving *inward* (deosil, clockwise), from the front door toward the center of the home, to draw new energy in. This isn't universal across every tradition, but it's a helpful structure if you don't already follow your own.

A simple sequence:

- **Clear first, clean second.** Remove anything broken, unused, or that no longer serves you before you so much as pick up a rag.
- **Wipe down entry points** — front door, doorknobs, windowsills — with a simple saltwater or vinegar rinse if that's part of your practice. Keep this pass strictly for banishing; avoid mixing vinegar with any sweet or herbal drawing wash in the same wipe-down, since the two are doing opposite work.
- **Sweep outward**, from the back of the home toward the front door, visualizing stagnant energy moving out with the dust — then carry it fully across the threshold onto the porch or straight into a dustpan taken immediately outside. Leaving swept dust sitting inside the home, even in a trash can, is said to undo the banishing pass; tradition holds that it needs to cross the home's boundary completely.
- **Open a window**, even briefly, once you're done. Let the space breathe before you bring anything new in.

Only once a space feels clear — and the banishing pass has fully dried — should you move on to inviting something in.

## Part Two: Botanicals, Spices & Herbs for Prosperity

This is where the ritual turns from clearing to *drawing in*. Certain herbs and spices have long associations with luck, abundance, and financial flow — and the beauty of this step is that most of them are already sitting in your kitchen cabinet.

A few staples worth knowing:

- **Cinnamon** — associated with fast luck and quick money; a classic choice for doorways and thresholds.
- **Basil** — tied to prosperity and protecting wealth once it arrives.
- **Bay leaves** — traditionally used for wishes; some practitioners write an intention on a leaf before burning it.
- **Cloves** — linked to both luck and protection, often added to sachets.
- **Mint** — considered money-drawing; frequently placed near entryways or in wallets.
- **Orange peel** — connected to abundance and solar energy, fitting for the last warm light of the season.

A few ways to put these to use:

- **Simmer pot.** Combine cinnamon sticks, cloves, dried orange peel, and a bay leaf or two in a small pot of water. Let it simmer on low — it scents the home while the ritual does its quiet work in the background. (If you have curious pets, keep simmer pots out of reach and stick to sachets rather than loose herbs scattered on the floor — cinnamon, cloves, and mint can irritate paws and airways, and concentrated forms are toxic to cats and dogs.)
- **Prosperity sachet.** Fill a small cloth pouch with a pinch of each herb and tuck it near your front door or under your welcome mat.
- **Threshold sprinkle & sweep.** Standing at your front door, blow or sprinkle a small pinch of dried cinnamon or mint just inside the threshold while speaking a short incantation — something like *"With this spice I welcome ease, abundance carried in on the breeze,"* or your own words if you have them. Sweep it up right after into a dustpan and set it aside to discard normally — since this is a *drawing* action, skip the outward-across-the-threshold disposal from the banishing step above; that route is for expelling, not for herbs meant to invite something in. This keeps loose herbs off the floor for more than a moment — a good option if you have pets — while still giving the moment real ritual weight.
- **Prosperity jar.** Layer herbs in a small jar with a coin or two, seal it, and set it somewhere it won't be disturbed until the next season turns.

None of this requires elaborate tools. The intention behind the action matters more than the setup.

## Part Three: The Transition Decor

Once the clearing and inviting-in feels complete, decorating becomes less about aesthetics alone and more like sealing the ritual in place — a visible, daily reminder of the intention you've set.

Many people find it works best to decorate in two waves rather than one. First, lean into cozy autumn: warm-toned textiles, dried florals, pumpkins and gourds, candles in amber and rust tones. This is the "harvest" layer — abundant, warm, grounded. Later in the season, as the air turns and Halloween approaches, layer in the darker, more atmospheric pieces — the moody florals, the candlelight, the touches that shift the space from harvest table to something a little more witchy.

A nice ritual note: consider decorating your entryway last, once everything else is done. Since it's the threshold you cleared and blessed first, finishing there closes the loop — the space that welcomed prosperity in is now the space that visibly announces the season has arrived. To mark the moment, try lighting a beeswax votive on your entryway console table as the final step — a small, tactile way of "sealing" the working once the decor is in place.

## A Simple Autumn Prosperity Checklist

If you want the condensed version, this allows you to check the steps off as you go:

- [ ] Clear clutter, starting at the entryway
- [ ] Wipe down doorways and thresholds
- [ ] Sweep from back of home toward the front door
- [ ] Open a window to let the space reset
- [ ] Simmer or sachet your prosperity herbs (cinnamon, basil, bay, cloves, mint, orange peel)
- [ ] Sprinkle or blow cinnamon/mint at the threshold with an incantation, then sweep up
- [ ] Place a sachet or jar near your entry
- [ ] Decorate in cozy-autumn tones first
- [ ] Layer in Halloween decor as the season deepens
- [ ] Finish decorating at the entryway to seal the ritual

This ritual isn't about perfection — it's about intention. Some years you'll do the full version, candles and simmer pots and all. Other years it might be a five-minute sweep and a sprinkle of cinnamon by the door. Both count. The goal is simply to greet the harvest season with a home, and a mind, that's ready to receive it.
